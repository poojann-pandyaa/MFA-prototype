Adaptive MFA

Risk-based multi-factor authentication for bank payments — the right amount of proof for every transaction
Project presentation · November 2026
The problem

One OTP for everything is both too weak and too annoying

How banks confirm payments today

  • The same SMS code for ₹50 of groceries and a ₹5 lakh transfer
  • OTPs are stolen through SIM swaps, phishing pages and malware
  • No look at who is paying or whether the request is normal for this customer
  • Scam victims are talked into reading out the code themselves

What we set out to build

  • Light checks when a payment looks normal for this customer
  • Strong checks (face, voice, behaviour) when it looks risky
  • Money moves only with a proof tied to that exact amount and payee
  • A platform any bank can plug in, with its own policy
Adaptive MFA scores every sensitive request, chooses the checks, runs them on the customer's own phone, and returns a signed, single-use approval.

4
Deciding the checks

Three inputs, the strictest one wins

1 · AI risk model

~38 signals compared with the customer's own last 90 days: amount vs. usual, new payee / phone / place, odd hour, recent SIM or password change, failed checks.

2 · Bank policy

Each bank sets a minimum per action — a password change always needs more than a small bill.

3 · Regulatory minimum

At least two kinds of proof, one of them "live" (code, face or voice).

LevelMeaningChecks asked
T0Very ordinaryOne-time code
T1Ordinary, light checkCode + typing behaviour
T2Somewhat unusual+ face check, scam warning
T3Unusual+ voice check
T4SevereEvery check the customer has set up

If a needed check isn't set up, face and voice stand in for each other. A customer is never queued for a person just because they haven't added voice or typing.

5
Features

The five ways a customer proves it's them

🔢

One-time code

Email, SMS or authenticator app. 6 digits, 3-minute expiry, the message names the amount and payee.

🙂

Face

Face match (YuNet + SFace) and liveness models that catch photos, screens and replays. A random head turn defeats pre-recorded video.

🎙️

Voice

Speaker match (ECAPA), a deepfake detector (RawNet2), and speech recognition (Vosk) to confirm the random words were said.

⌨️

Typing behaviour

How the customer types a phrase and traces a line, scored by an anomaly model (Isolation Forest). Separate keyboard and touch profiles.

📱

Phone key

Fingerprint or screen lock signs AMFA's challenge with a key in the phone's secure hardware.

Progressive setup

Sign-up asks only for the face (about a minute). Voice, typing and an authenticator are added later from Security — each one means fewer interruptions.

Failures

Ordinary mistakes only use up retries. Repeated fake-face or fake-voice flags decline the request and lock the account until a reviewer clears it.

6
Security

Why an approval can't be forged, replayed or reused

Signed proof

  • Bound to the exact amount and payee
  • Valid ~2 minutes, single use
  • Changing anything afterwards voids it

End-to-end encrypted biometrics

  • Phone encrypts face / voice / typing to AMFA's key (P-256 + AES-256-GCM)
  • Each box tied to its session and challenge
  • The bank never sees a frame or recording

Isolated services

  • Services talk via signed (HMAC) requests
  • Templates live only in their own service
  • Hash-chained, tamper-evident audit log

Device trust

A new phone must pass strong checks before banking. A trusted phone later proves itself with a key it keeps.

Customer controls

"Freeze my account" blocks everything. "This wasn't me" raises an alert and records a fraud label.

Data kept 90 days

Transactions are purged after 90 days; the risk model never reads further back.

7
Customer experience

Keeping everyday payments light

Automatic reliefs (switched off on any warning sign)

  • Small payments under the bank's low band stay at a light level
  • Regular payees — rent, bills, the same shop — stay light even at a higher amount
  • Own habit — a weekly ₹1 lakh to a wholesaler is routine; ₹10,000 from someone who pays ₹1,500 is a jump
  • Mule payees reported by others are flagged for every customer

New customers and optional controls

  • Learning phase: until the model knows a new customer, the phone's fingerprint confirms each payment
  • Two optional sign-up questions (typical payment, monthly total) give a head start
  • "No extra checks up to ₹X a day" set by the customer, never below the legal minimum
  • Family helper: register a relative's phone that helps with payments
Large first payments to a new payee always show a scam warning — biometrics prove identity, not that the customer isn't being coached.
8
Human review

A person is called in only when checks can't settle it

Held for a reviewer

  • Payee is on a risk list
  • SIM, contact or password changed recently, then money to a new payee (takeover + cash-out)
  • Suspected fake face or recording
  • Customer on the bank's watchlist
  • The legal minimum can't be met with the checks the customer has

How a held request proceeds

  • Customer first completes every check they can
  • Two different reviewers must approve (four-eyes)
  • Approval allowed only if a live check passed
  • Rejected cases become fraud labels for retraining
  • The customer can cancel at any time

A high threat level alone is answered with the strongest automated checks, not a queue.

9
The AI inside

AI judges and recognises; rules decide

Risk model

  • Ensemble of 5 gradient-boosted tree models (LightGBM), calibrated to a fraud probability
  • One-way constraints: a bigger amount can never look safer
  • Level cut-offs set by a "friction budget" of genuine traffic
  • Every decision logged with its top reasons

Trained on a fraud simulator

  • 20,000 customers × 150 days ≈ 1.2 million requests
  • 14 customer types, 47 genuine look-alike situations
  • 25 attack types: account takeover, SIM swap, coached scams, mules, bots, slow drip…
  • Must beat a logistic-regression baseline before release

Local language model (Qwen2.5-3B)

Runs on the server, sees only coarse flags, may add a check — never remove one.

Band switch

The model earns the right to lower checks per amount band, only after real outcomes prove it, with two operators approving.

Honest limits

Limitations and next steps

Known limitations

  • Trained on simulated data — no real fraud labels yet
  • Fairness gate fails for older customers (step-up ratio ~2.3 vs. limit 1.25)
  • Coached scams: the real customer passes every check
  • Low-and-slow attacks are caught less often
  • Web check pages don't yet encrypt end to end

Next steps

  • Retrain on real reviewer verdicts and fraud reports
  • Rebalance training to fix the fairness gap
  • Scam signals: active call, screen sharing, pasted account numbers
  • Pick the lightest checks that meet the risk target
  • Device attestation; encryption in the web SDK
